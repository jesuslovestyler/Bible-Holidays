The holidays, one by one

The seven appointed feasts of Leviticus 23 — with the Sabbath that stands at their head — and the two later days Scripture itself celebrates: Purim and the Feast of Dedication.

The Sabbath

Shabbat · שבת
Weekly
Every seventh day — Friday sundown to Saturday nightfall

The first holy day named in Leviticus 23, before all the feasts: a day of complete rest, remembering both creation (Exodus 20:8–11) and the exodus from Egypt (Deuteronomy 5:15).

In Jesus“The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath. Therefore the Son of Man is lord even of the Sabbath.” — Mark 2:27–28 (WEB). The letter to the Hebrews holds out a greater rest still to come (Hebrews 4:9–11).

Passover

Pesach · פסח
Spring
14 Nisan

The night the Lord passed over the houses marked with the lamb's blood and brought Israel out of Egypt. The Passover meal is eaten after sundown, as 15 Nisan begins.

In JesusJesus ate this meal with his disciples the night before the cross (Matthew 26:17–30). Paul names the meaning plainly: “Christ, our Passover, has been sacrificed” — 1 Corinthians 5:7 (WEB).

Unleavened Bread

Chag HaMatzot · חג המצות
Spring
15–21 Nisan · seven days

The week straight after Passover: bread made without leaven, because Israel left Egypt in haste — and because leaven is to be put out of the house, and out of the life, altogether.

In JesusPaul keeps the picture: purge out the old leaven, “for indeed Christ, our Passover, has been sacrificed in our place” — and keep the feast “with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth” (1 Corinthians 5:7–8, WEB).

Read: Leviticus 23:6–8 · Exodus 12:15–20

Firstfruits

Yom HaBikkurim · יום הביכורים
Spring
16 Nisan · the barley sheaf

The priest waves the first sheaf of the barley harvest before the Lord — the first of the crop, given to God, as a promise that the rest of the harvest is coming. The counting of the fifty days to Weeks begins here.

In Jesus“Christ has been raised from the dead. He became the first fruits of those who are asleep.” — 1 Corinthians 15:20 (WEB). His resurrection fell in these very days of the feast.
Friday, April 23, 2027Begins at sundown Thu, Apr 22
Read: Leviticus 23:9–14

Feast of Weeks

Shavuot · שבועות · Pentecost
Spring
6 Sivan · fifty days after Firstfruits

Count seven weeks from the sheaf, then bring the firstfruits of the wheat harvest — two leavened loaves waved before the Lord. By tradition this is also the season Israel received the Torah at Sinai.

In JesusOn this very feast in Jerusalem, the Holy Spirit came with wind and fire and the church was born: “When the day of Pentecost had come… they were all filled with the Holy Spirit” — Acts 2:1–4 (WEB).
Friday, June 11, 2027Begins at sundown Thu, Jun 10
Read: Leviticus 23:15–22 · Acts 2

Feast of Trumpets

Yom Teruah · יום תרועה · Rosh Hashanah
Fall
1 Tishrei · a memorial of blowing of trumpets

A solemn rest heralded by the blast of the shofar — a wake-up call that opens the fall feasts and, by tradition, the new year. Ten days of reflection follow, leading to the Day of Atonement.

In JesusThe trumpet sounds through the New Testament at the Lord's return: “the trumpet of God” (1 Thessalonians 4:16, WEB) and “the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound and the dead will be raised incorruptible” (1 Corinthians 15:52, WEB).

Read: Leviticus 23:23–25 · Numbers 29:1

Day of Atonement

Yom Kippur · יום כיפור
Fall
10 Tishrei · the holiest day of the year

Once a year the high priest alone entered the Most Holy Place with blood, to make atonement for the whole people. A day of fasting and afflicting the soul — the most solemn day on the calendar.

In JesusHebrews reads this day as a shadow of Christ, our high priest, who entered “once for all into the Holy Place… having obtained eternal redemption” — Hebrews 9:12 (WEB); see Hebrews 9–10.
Monday, October 11, 2027Fast begins at sundown Sun, Oct 10
Read: Leviticus 16 · Leviticus 23:26–32

Feast of Tabernacles

Sukkot · סוכות · Booths
Fall
15–21 Tishrei · seven days

The joyful harvest feast: live for a week in booths of branches, remembering the wilderness journey — that God sheltered Israel under open skies, and that He delights to dwell among his people.

In JesusJesus went up to this feast and cried out in the temple, “If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink!” (John 7:2, 37–38, WEB). The story ends where Sukkot always pointed: “God's dwelling is with people” — Revelation 21:3 (WEB).
Sat Oct 16 – Fri Oct 22, 2027Begins at sundown Fri, Oct 15
Read: Leviticus 23:33–43 · John 7

The Eighth Day

Shemini Atzeret · שמיני עצרת
Fall
22 Tishrei · the day after Tabernacles

“On the eighth day you shall have a holy convocation” — one final, smaller day of assembly after the week of booths, closing the whole feast season before the rains and the new year of work.

In JesusIt was “on the last and greatest day of the feast” that Jesus stood and cried out his promise of living water (John 7:37, WEB) — the feast's last days and this day stand side by side in his teaching.
Saturday, October 23, 2027Begins at sundown Fri, Oct 22
Read: Leviticus 23:36, 39 · Numbers 29:35

Purim

Purim · פורים · “Lots”
More days
14 Adar · 14 Adar II in a leap year

Remembering Esther: how Haman cast lots (purim) for Israel's destruction, and how God turned the day around — “the reverse happened,” and sorrow became joy. Celebrated with the reading of Esther, gifts of food, and gifts to the poor.

In ScriptureThe book of Esther never speaks God's name aloud — yet his hand is on every page, preserving his people in exile. The feast was established by Mordecai and Esther themselves (Esther 9:20–32).
Tuesday, March 23, 2027Begins at sundown Mon, Mar 22
Read: The book of Esther, especially ch. 9

Feast of Dedication

Chanukah · חנוכה · Hanukkah
More days
25 Kislev – 2 Tevet · eight days

Eight nights remembering the rededication of the Temple under the Maccabees, after it had been defiled — the lampstand lit again in God's house, in the darkest weeks of the year.

In Jesus“It was the Feast of the Dedication at Jerusalem. It was winter, and Jesus was walking in the temple, in Solomon's porch.” — John 10:22–23 (WEB). He kept this feast in Jerusalem himself.

How to read this calendar

Each day begins at sundown

“From evening to evening, you shall keep your Sabbath” (Leviticus 23:32, WEB). So every date on this page is the feast's first full day — and each card tells you the evening before, when it actually begins.

Why the dates move

The Hebrew calendar follows the moon, so a feast is fixed on its Hebrew date — Passover is always 14 Nisan — but lands on a different day of our calendar each year. This page converts for you: open it any day, in any year, and the “next” dates and countdowns are worked out fresh from today's date.

Leap years add a month

To keep Passover in spring, a second month of Adar is added seven times in nineteen years. In those years Purim is kept in Adar II — which is why Purim 2027 falls on March 23, in Adar II 5787.
